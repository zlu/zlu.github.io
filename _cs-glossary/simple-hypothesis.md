A **simple hypothesis** completely specifies the distribution: e.g. $H_0:\theta=\theta_0$ with no free nuisance parameters left. A [composite](/cs-glossary/composite-hypothesis/) hypothesis leaves a set of $\theta$ values (e.g. $H_1:\theta>\theta_0$).

Neyman–Pearson lemma gives most powerful tests between two simple hypotheses; composite cases need UMP / likelihood-ratio constructions.
